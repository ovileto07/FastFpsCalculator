What affects your FPS?

GPU performance

Your graphics card is usually the main factor at higher resolutions and visual quality settings.

CPU performance

The processor can become the limiting component in CPU-heavy games and when targeting very high frame rates.

Resolution & settings

1080p, 1440p and 4K place different demands on the GPU, while quality and ray tracing can change the workload substantially.

How to get a useful FPS estimate

1. Select your exact hardware

Choose the CPU and GPU that are actually installed in your PC. Different generations and models can have large performance differences.

2. Select the game

Game engines and workloads vary. A competitive game can behave very differently from a demanding open-world or ray-traced title.

3. Select resolution and quality

Use the resolution and graphics preset closest to the way you normally play. Upscaling and ray tracing can also affect the result.

4. Treat the result as an estimate

Real FPS can change with drivers, patches, RAM configuration, temperatures, background processes and the exact scene in the game. The calculator is intended as a practical performance estimate, not a guaranteed benchmark.
